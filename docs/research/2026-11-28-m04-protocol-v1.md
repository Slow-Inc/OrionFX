## 1. What the research changed (summary)

- **Accuracy and break-even.** Credible out-of-sample directional accuracy for intraday FX, gold and equities ML is about **50–55%**. With stressed costs and ATR(M15) of $3–6, the break-even hit rate is about 54–59% (FLAT share up to 30%), and higher under stressed cost. A fixed "≥ 53% accuracy" target is therefore not an economic gate.
- **Three-class edge.** `(2p−1)` overstates the edge when FLAT has probability mass. Under the tb.v2 label (§2), EV = size·(d·(p_side − p_opp) − (spread + 2·slip)) − commission.
- **Labels are a proxy for real exits.** The system has **no per-position SL/TP** (master plan RSK-05). Its real exits are the Emergency SL, the USD cycle target and basket trailing. The ±1·ATR triple-barrier label is a proxy. Only the **system tier** (#131), which replays the real exits, can show that a model is usable.
- **Sample size.**
  - 200 trades lets luck through. With no skill, the chance that one of 20 configs reaches PF ≥ 1.15 is 16–61%.
  - A deflated Sharpe ≥ 0.95 across 20 configs at a per-trade Sharpe of about 0.05–0.07 needs thousands of out-of-sample trades.
  - The calculations were re-run: `calc.py`, reproduced.
- **Live haircut.** Expect live Sharpe at about half of backtest. A backtest Sharpe barely predicts live Sharpe (Wiecki et al.: R² < 0.025).
- **Geometry sets the class balance (model-dev research F3).** At k = 1·ATR_M15 a driftless model gives a FLAT share of about 0.78 at an M15 horizon and about 0.19 at H1. H1 is the natural horizon for k = 1.
- **A binned ECE ≤ 0.02 fails on noise alone (model-dev research F4).** At the per-fold sample size, a perfectly calibrated model shows a binned ECE of about 0.028. The calibration gate is restated in §5.

**Expected outcome, stated now.** The research puts realistic accuracy at or below break-even, so the **likeliest v1 result is "did not meet targets"**. With the fixed-dollar `STRESSED_COST` ($1.07/oz), V2 needs ATR_M15 ≥ $10.70/k on the most recent development era (§6). So "non-viable", with 0 configs spent, is also a live outcome. Under the goal, an honest failure is a complete result, just as a pass would be.

## 1a. Data source

- **Planned training corpus:** FirstRate Data **gold futures (GC), 1-minute bars, 2008 to present**. Status: **PENDING OWNER PURCHASE**. The licence permits:
  - import into databases;
  - use in internal models;
  - hosting at a service provider (Colab).
- **What the source is.** GC prices are **last trade**, not bid. For this source, the tb.v2 barriers (§2) therefore run on the **trade path**. GC is a **proxy** for the broker's spot XAUUSD feed. It differs from that feed in three ways:
  - it has contract rolls;
  - it has a basis gap to spot;
  - it has no bid and no spread.
- **How the protocol handles the differences:**
  - **Costs.** They are applied only in EV and the backtest, through the pre-declared spot cost model `STRESSED_COST` (§5). They are never taken from the source.
  - **Features.** Spread and volume features are excluded for this source. GC has no spread, and exchange volume means something different from broker tick volume.
  - **Rolls.** The #128 import (`origin/feat/128-historical-import` at b21292c) uses FirstRate's **ratio-adjusted** continuous GC series (`docs/research/2026-11-28-xauusd-data-sources.md` §5, on that branch).
    - Ratio adjustment multiplies a whole segment by one factor, so returns, ATR-normalised values and other scale-free features (§4) are unchanged.
    - **Rows whose label window crosses a roll are therefore not excluded.** The remaining artefact is FirstRate's small residual at the join.
    - Roll dates and the cumulative roll factor f(t) ≤ 1 are recorded per dataset version in `candle_rolls`.
  - **Price units.** Adjusted prices are not tradable levels. Older history carries f(t), so anything in price units must be scaled by f(t) on adjusted data (§5).
- **Scope of the claim.** A pass on GC is a claim **about GC**. Before any Shadow promotion, a pre-declared **spot-feed parity check** must pass. It runs on the broker's MT5 XAUUSD data (#17) or on a licensed spot source, over a spot window that no v1 run has read. The window holds ≥ 3 months and enough n_eff for the G2 power check (§5). The selected model is scored on that window with tb.v2 on the spot bid path. It passes only if all three hold:
  - **Feature parity:** across the selected model's features, the median PSI between GC-derived and spot-derived features is ≤ 0.10, and no feature exceeds 0.25;
  - **Brier:** the spot Brier skill point estimate is > 0, and its day-block LB is > −0.005;
  - **ECE:** G2(a) holds on the spot window, against its own noise floor.

  The GC/spot label agreement rate on overlapping rows is reported as a diagnostic. If parity fails, there is no Shadow promotion. The result stays "passed on GC, not shown on spot".

Dukascopy XAUUSD becomes the **primary corpus** and HistData the fallback; no purchase is needed. The rejection below is kept as history. This section is to be reconciled with that decision.
- **Rejected sources (before the owner decision):**
  - **Restrictive terms:** Dukascopy, Polygon/Massive, Twelve Data and Databento.
  - **Unclear terms or doubtful provenance:** HistData, OANDA, Stooq, and the Kaggle and Hugging Face sets.

  Details: `docs/research/2026-11-28-dukascopy-terms.md`, and the data-sources note being added on `feat/128-historical-import`.

**Certification:** certified by agent under owner delegation, 2026-11-28 (the source choice itself waits for the owner's purchase). **Rationale:**
- GC is the only option found whose licence covers internal model use and hosting at Colab.
- The parity check keeps the GC→spot gap explicit, so it cannot be assumed away.
- **PSI 0.10 / 0.25.** These are the credit-scorecard convention: below 0.10 no significant shift, 0.10–0.25 moderate, above 0.25 significant (Siddiqi 2006, *Credit Risk Scorecards*, (nf)). The kill switch in §9 already uses 0.25.
  - The median of ≤ 0.10 requires the typical feature to show no significant shift.
  - The 0.25 ceiling refuses any feature that shifted significantly.
  - Evidence: [L]. It is an industry convention, not a study of GC vs spot.
- **Spot Brier: point estimate > 0 and LB > −0.005.**
  - Targets research §2 expects the true Brier skill to be tiny, about 0.001–0.01 [L].
  - A ≥ 3-month spot window cannot resolve that, so the LB tolerance is set at half the upper end of that range: −0.005. It allows for spot-window noise.
  - The point-estimate condition still refuses negative skill.
  - Evidence: [L], derived.

## 2. Label: `m07.labels.tb.v2` (implemented in #129)

- **One exhaustive label per row:** UP, DOWN or FLAT.
  - Barriers are symmetric at ±d, where d = k·ATR_M15 and k = `TARGET_ATR`. ATR_M15 is read at the decision bar and frozen.
  - k is chosen in the pre-run step (§6) from {1.0, 1.25, 1.5, 2.0}. It is recorded in the label version, for example `m07.labels.tb.v2.k150` for k = 1.5.
  - Touches are measured on the **bid** path. For the GC training source, which has last-trade prices only, they use the trade path (§1a).
  - UP means +d is touched first and DOWN means −d is touched first. FLAT means neither is touched within the horizon.
- **No costs inside the label.** There is no spread and no slippage in the barriers. Costs live in one place: EV and the backtest, where they can be stressed.
- **Dual touch.** A bar that touches both barriers cannot be ordered, so the row is **ambiguous and excluded** from training and evaluation. It is not FLAT.
  - Touches are resolved on the finest bars tb.v2 uses (M1 where the source has it).
  - The dual-touch rate is reported **per era** (and per session).
  - The exclusion biases the sample toward calmer paths, and the rate makes that bias visible.
- **EV that prices this label:**

  ```
  EV = size · (d · (p_side − p_opp) − (spread + 2·slip)) − commission
  ```

  - The required edge is p_side − p_opp > cost/d, where cost/d = (spread + 2·slip + commission/size) / d.
  - A FLAT is taken as zero gross P&L, but it still pays the costs. The walk-forward reports the realised mean FLAT payoff in units of d, per era, as a diagnostic.
- **Why tb.v1 (`m07.labels.tb.v1`) was rejected.** It was **side-dependent** and **spread-dependent**.
  - *Side-dependent.* It gave one label per side, with the spread paid inside the trigger. So a long loss was not the same event as a short win, and a "both lose" band appeared. The labels were then not exhaustive, and p_side − p_opp was not the long payoff (model-dev research F2). Its "dual touch counts as SL first" rule is also per side, and cannot apply to one three-class label.
  - *Spread-dependent.* The label moved with the source's spread definition: Dukascopy gives a close-to-close spread, while the live candle stores the max spread in the bar. A missing spread was also charged a constant. That is a train/serve skew built into the target itself.

**Certification:** certified by agent under owner delegation, 2026-11-28. **Rationale:** with one exhaustive bid-path label, p_up − p_down is exactly the payoff driver of a symmetric ±d bet. The label does not depend on any source's spread definition. Every cost sits in one stressable place.

| Window | Rule | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Debug window | The first 12 months of available history: **2009-01-01 → 2010-01-01 UTC** (HistData XAUUSD M1 starts in 2009; see the decision under the holdout rule). | Free to read, for code debugging only. | Walk-forward | After the debug window, up to the holdout start. | All configs are judged here. Layout per fold:<br>- expanding training, ≥ 3 years at the first fold;<br>- purge ≥ 1 label horizon;<br>- a 3-month calibration slice;<br>- purge ≥ 1 label horizon;<br>- a 6-month test fold.<br>The step is 6 months, and purging is by label end (`ends_at`). **Holdout length rule.** The length is fixed at hash time, from facts known then. It is never sized from walk-forward results.
- L = the span expected to hold **1,260 trades** at the **planning trade rate of 2 trades a day**, which is **30 months**.
- L is never below 12 months. It is shortened only if the walk-forward would otherwise keep less than **3.6 years** of pooled out-of-sample test folds (MinBTL at N = 20, SR 1; targets research §3.1).
- **Why the planning rate, not a measured one.** No model may run on development data before the hash. The 2008 debug window says nothing about the trade rate in a recent holdout, especially at a fixed-dollar stressed cost (§6).
- **Where the numbers come from:**
  - **1,260 trades:** targets research §3.1. It is the number of trades that gives 80% power to confirm the PF 1.15 reference effect (per-trade SR 0.07) at N = 1: ((1.645 + 0.842)/0.07)² ≈ 1,262 (`calc.py`).
  - **2 trades a day:** the lower end of the 2–4 trades a day that targets research §3.1 assumes. Taking the low end gives the longer, more conservative holdout. 1,260 / (2 × 252) × 12 = **30 months**.
- At 3 and 4 trades a day, 1,260 trades would take 20 and 15 months.
- If the real rate is lower, the power check (§8.4) reports "inconclusive", rather than opening an underpowered holdout.
- **Dates:** fixed below (2026-11-28) by this rule. The hashed record carries them.

**Minimum trade count, checked before opening.** The power check (§8) recomputes the minimum on DSR terms, with N = counted configs actually run:

```
T_min (trading days) = κ · ((E_N + z_0.95 + z_0.80) / SR_d)²,     κ = 1 − γ3·SR_d + (γ4 − 1)/4 · SR_d²
minimum trade count  = T_min × (walk-forward trades per trading day)
```

- **E_N** is the expected-maximum factor at N trials: (1−γ)Φ⁻¹(1−1/N) + γΦ⁻¹(1−1/(N·e)). It is 0 at N = 1, 1.05 at N = 4 and 1.90 at N = 20.
- **SR_d** is the selected config's walk-forward daily Sharpe at stressed cost. γ3 and γ4 are its skew and kurtosis. z_0.95 = 1.645 and z_0.80 = 0.842.
- This is MinTRL (Bailey & López de Prado 2012) set against the DSR benchmark, using the null V[SR] = 1/T simplification as in targets research §3.1.
- Reference values in per-trade terms (independent trades), from `calc.py`:

  | Per-trade SR | N = 1 | N = 4 | N = 20 |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 0.07 | 1,262 | 2,556 | 3,929 |
  | 0.10 | 619 | 1,253 | 1,925 |

- If the fixed holdout cannot be expected to reach the minimum, it is **not opened**, and the result is **inconclusive (underpowered)**.

**Certification:** certified by agent under owner delegation, 2026-11-28. **Rationale:**
- The draft fixed **18 months**, argued from power at N = 1. That no longer drives the length, for three reasons:
  - power now gates *opening* at the stricter N = configs-run minimum, so an underpowered holdout ends as "inconclusive", never as a false pass;
  - a power-driven length cannot be fixed before results without assuming the effect size;
  - every month moved into the holdout leaves the walk-forward, where the 20-config selection needs MinBTL.
- The 12-month floor follows MinTRL. At a daily SR of 0.1, rejecting SR ≤ 0 takes about 270 trading days (model-dev research §2.5), so a shorter holdout cannot pass a meaningful gate.
- F now depends on the corpus and the holdout length. From a 2008 start it is about 27, not the draft's 15, so the G1 sign test is restated as a rule on the actual F (§5).

## 4. Pipeline requirements (fixed; not configs)

These are code-level correctness defaults from model-dev research §9(A). They are not selection choices, so they spend no budget. A config may not switch one off. Tags are the research's evidence levels.

**Data and features**
1. **As-of joins on bar close** [H]. Every higher-timeframe bar is keyed by `close_time = open_time + duration` and joined with a backward as-of merge on `close_time ≤ decision time`. A partial current bar is built from M1 up to t only, as a separate feature.
2. **Trailing-only statistics** [H]. Windows end at t on closed bars, with `min_periods` equal to the full window, computed causally on the continuous series. Not allowed: centred windows, backfill, full-sample scalers, and expanding statistics over train plus test. There is no global z-scoring.
3. **Leak tests** [H]:
   - a unit test that `max(feature source timestamp) ≤ as_of` for every row;
   - a future-feature canary (`close[t+1]` as a feature), which must produce a large skill jump and is then removed.
4. **Scale-free features only** [H]: divided by ATR or price, log returns, ratios, or trailing ranks and percentiles. No dollar levels. Spread and volume features are excluded for the GC source (§1a). For any other source they are excluded, or self-normalised per source against the same source's trailing median for the same hour of the week.
5. **Sample hygiene** [M]:
   - horizons are counted in trading bars of the source's `SessionSchedule`;
   - rows whose label window crosses the daily break or a weekend are dropped;
   - rollover rows (17:00 NY ± 15 min) are excluded from training and from the trading mask;
   - **gaps** (#128 data-sources note §5 item 2):
     - a run of up to **15** missing open-session minutes between two traded minutes is "no trade". It is filled with synthetic flat bars at the previous close (volume 0, `NO_TRADE` quality flag), and **labels may span these fills**;
     - a longer run is a **real gap** (`GAP`), and a row whose label window contains a real gap gets **no label**.
6. **Sampling** [M]: one row per **M15 close**. The ECE figures assume this. If the system tier (#131) cannot query the model at M15 closes only, sampling moves to every M5 close with uniqueness weights. That fallback is **TBD before hash**.
7. **Uniqueness weights** [M]. `sample_weight` is the average uniqueness: the mean of 1/concurrency over the label span. Claim metrics are pre-registered:
   - Brier skill is unweighted, on all out-of-sample rows, with a day-block bootstrap;
   - ECE is computed on the non-overlapping thinned sample (§5, G2).
8. **No class rebalancing** [M]: no SMOTE, no `is_unbalance` and no class weights. Geometry controls the FLAT share.

**Model**

9. **LightGBM, multiclass softmax, shallow and regularised** [H for the list; M for the values]:

   | Parameter | Value |
   |---|---|
   | `objective` | `multiclass` (softmax, not `multiclassova`) |
   | `num_leaves` | 15 |
   | `max_depth` | 4 |
   | `min_data_in_leaf` | 1% of the fold's training rows, minimum 500 |
   | `learning_rate` | 0.02 |
   | `feature_fraction` | 0.7 |
   | `bagging_fraction` / `bagging_freq` | 0.7 / 1 |
   | `lambda_l2` | 10 |
   | `max_bin` | 63 |
   | `extra_trees` / `path_smooth` | false / 0 |
   | monotone constraints | none |

10. **Early stopping** on a purged inner validation slice: the last 10% of training time, with metric `multi_logloss`. The early-stopped model is kept, with no refit.
11. **5 seeds averaged inside every config.** The seed list is part of the config hash. Seed averaging is variance reduction, not a trial.

**Calibration**

12. **Temperature scaling** [H for the method; M for the placement]. One temperature per fold, fitted on that fold's 3-month calibration slice, which sits after training and immediately before the test, purged by ≥ 1 label horizon on both sides.
    - It is never fitted on test data, or on data after the test.
    - It is not refitted inside a test fold.
    - Isotonic is not used in v1: the slice's n_eff is about 1.4k, far below the ~10k isotonic needs.

**Importance, statistics and controls**

13. **MDA / clustered MDA / SFI** [M/H]. MDA is permutation importance on purged out-of-fold slices, scored by log-loss. Clustered MDA permutes whole clusters of correlated features. SFI is the single-feature check. **No MDI.**
    - Feature selection is nested: it uses training-period folds only.
    - Selection on outer test folds counts as a config.
14. **PBO by CSCV** [H] on the T × N matrix of walk-forward out-of-sample **daily system P&L**, with S = 16 blocks (12,870 splits).
15. **DSR** [H] on **daily system P&L**, not per trade, because basket trades overlap.
    - N = the counted configs run (§8).
    - V[SR] is taken across all counted configs' out-of-sample Sharpe ratios.
    - Skew and kurtosis come from the series.
    - N_eff (ONC clusters) is reported but never used to loosen the gate.
16. **Non-ML controls** on the same folds: class priors, persistence, and a vol-only WAIT rule. They benchmark Brier skill and system expectancy. They are not trials.

**Out-of-distribution checks** [M]

17. `ModelCard.feature_ranges` is set to the training [0.5%, 99.5%] quantiles. Any feature out of range makes `predict` refuse, and M05 waits.
18. Per-feature PSI is used (already built).
19. Performance drift on resolved labels (CUSUM or Page–Hinkley on rolling Brier) feeds the ADR 0001 rollback. Its thresholds are [L] and are set in #133, not here.

**Certification:** certified by agent under owner delegation, 2026-11-28. **Rationale:**
- Each item is either a leak guard, with high evidence, or a fixed variance-reduction default with no tuning freedom.
- Fixing them before the first run takes the forks the research left open out of the budget:
  - the calibrator method and its placement;
  - the regularisation values;
  - early stopping.
- **`min_data_in_leaf` = 1% of the fold's training rows, minimum 500.**
  - Model-dev research §4.1 gives "~1–2% of the training rows (≈ 500–2000)".
  - We take the lower end of the percentage, because `num_leaves` 15 and `max_depth` 4 already limit the trees. The stronger end (× 2) is arm #9.
  - The research's lower absolute value, 500, is the floor for small early folds.
  - Evidence: [M].
- **Early stopping on the last 10% of training time.**
  - Model-dev research §4.1 gives 10–15%.
  - We take 10%, which keeps more of the most recent data for training. At ≥ 3 years of training it is still ≥ about 3.6 months of validation.
  - Keeping the early-stopped model, rather than refitting with `best_iter × 1.1`, avoids a second free parameter.
  - Evidence: [M].
- **CSCV with S = 16 blocks.**
  - Bailey et al. (2017) need an even S. Model-dev research §6.1 recommends 16, which gives C(16, 8) = 12,870 splits: enough for a stable PBO estimate at low cost.
  - With about 13 years of walk-forward test data from a 2008 start, each block holds about 200 trading days, enough for a per-block Sharpe.
  - Evidence: [H] for the method, [M] for S.

## 5. Binding gates (assumption-light; all must pass)

**How the bounds are computed.** All "lower bound" (LB) values are one-sided 95% **block bootstrap over trading days** (block length 5 days, 10,000 resamples), on the pooled walk-forward test predictions or trades.

**Stressed cost**, in tb.v2 EV terms:

```
EV_stressed = size · (d · (p_side − p_opp) − (s + 2·slip)) − commission
```

- **(s, slip, commission) = `STRESSED_COST`**, the pre-declared spot cost model in `app/core/bet.py` (#129).
- On the #129 branch today it is spread 0.80, slippage 0.10 per fill and commission 0.07 per oz round trip, all [PROPOSED] there. The hashed record pins the values in force at hash time.
- The draft's "era's 75th-percentile spread" form is gone: the GC source has no spread (§1a).
- **Fixed dollars, not scaled by price or ATR** (§6 gives the reasons).
- **On the ratio-adjusted GC series** (§1a), `STRESSED_COST` stays fixed in **real** dollars. It is applied in adjusted units as **$1.07·f(t)**, where f(t) ≤ 1 is the cumulative roll factor from `candle_rolls`. This follows #128's data-sources note §5 item 4: anything in price units is scaled by f(t) in older history. cost/d is therefore highest in the low-price early eras.
- The level is higher than the draft's: about 1.07 per oz, against about 0.57 before.
- **An era where the EV gate takes fewer than 300 trades is "not covered"**: it is reported, but it is neither a pass nor a fail.
- **No separate modelled cost in the backtest.** No spread is taken from memory. The backtest, the trade rate and the holdout length all use stressed cost. The Demo-measured cost for the §9 kill switch is **TBD until Demo**.

| # | Tier | Gate | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| G1 | Model | Multiclass Brier skill against training-fold priors, unweighted.<br>- pooled LB > 0; **and**<br>- at least k_F of the F folds are positive, where k_F is the smallest count with a one-sided sign-test p < 0.05 at the actual F (F = 10 → 9, 12 → 10, 13 → 10, 15 → 12, 17 → 13); **and**<br>- no fold is below −0.01. | "Every fold > 0" is too strict for noisy folds. The sign test plus the pooled LB is robust. Stating k_F as a rule keeps it right when the holdout length changes F. |
| G2 | Model | **Calibration**, with pre-registered estimator, sample and noise floor (below).<br>(a) Pooled out-of-sample: ECE_obs ≤ q50(ECE_0) + **0.02**.<br>(b) Per-fold sanity bound: every fold's ECE_obs ≤ q50(that fold's ECE_0) + **0.04**.<br>(c) Traded region: on traded predictions, the realised edge's LB is above stressed cost/d within the traded probability range. | EV depends on calibrated p. A binned ECE ≤ 0.02 fails a perfectly calibrated model at the per-fold n_eff (about 0.028 from noise alone at n_eff ≈ 3k). So the gate is set as an excess over the noise floor at the actual n_eff. The 0.02 margin keeps the draft's materiality: an ECE of about 0.03 erases most of a 0.05–0.10 edge. (b) catches a model that is calibrated on average but broken in one era. (c) checks where the money is. |
| G3 | System (#131, real exits) | Net expectancy per trade: LB > 0 at stressed cost. | The economic gate. |
| G4 | System | Deflated Sharpe ratio ≥ 0.95, on **daily** system P&L, with N = **counted configs run** (the budget counter, §8). | Corrects for selection across the budget. Daily P&L avoids overstating T with overlapping basket trades. |
| G5 | System (walk-forward only) | PBO ≤ 0.2, by CSCV over the T × N matrix of walk-forward out-of-sample daily P&L (S = 16). Not applicable when N = 1; DSR is then the only selection guard. | Probability of backtest overfitting. It is meaningless on a single-config holdout. |
| G6 | Coverage | No adequately sized regime bucket (§7) is significantly negative: a one-sided test of expectancy < 0, Holm-corrected across buckets, p < 0.05. | Coverage of market conditions. |

**G2: estimator, sample and noise floor** (pre-registered; Roelofs et al. 2022):
- **Estimator:** class-wise **ECE_sweep**, the mean over UP, DOWN and FLAT. Each class uses equal-mass bins, with the largest bin count that keeps the bin-wise calibration curve monotone. The 15-bin debiased binned ECE (Bröcker 2012; Ferro & Fricker 2012) is reported as a diagnostic only.
- **Sample:** the non-overlapping thinned sample. Starting at each fold's first test row, keep one row per label horizon (every 4th M15 row at H1). **n_eff** is the number of rows kept. The other phase offsets are reported as diagnostics.
- **Noise floor:** ECE_0 is the distribution of the same estimator under **consistency resampling**.
  - Each row's label is redrawn from the model's own calibrated distribution: 1,000 draws, seeded, at the same rows and n_eff.
  - q50 is its median.
  - Approximate binned-estimator floors (model-dev research F4) are about 0.028 at n_eff 3k (one fold), about 0.020 at 6k (a 12-month holdout) and about 0.008 at 36k (about 12 folds pooled; a 2008 start gives more folds and a lower floor). The sweep estimator's floor is lower. The actual n_eff and floors are **TBD until the data arrives**, and are computed at run time.
- **Power check for G2**, run before the holdout opens, at the holdout's expected n_eff:
  - (i) with the selected config's calibrated probabilities taken as the truth, G2(a) passes in ≥ 95% of draws;
  - (ii) with the probabilities sharpened by a temperature until the true class-wise excess ECE is 0.04, G2(a) fails in ≥ 80% of draws.
  - If either fails, the holdout is underpowered for calibration and is not opened.

**On the holdout**, the gates that apply are:
- G1 (pooled LB > 0 only, since there are no folds);
- G2(a) and G2(c);
- G3;
- G4, with N = counted configs run;
- G6.

**Diagnostics** (reported, **not** gating, because the evidence for their levels is thin):
- direction accuracy on non-FLAT outcomes;
- precision and coverage by probability band;
- profit factor (reference 1.15 at stressed cost in walk-forward, 1.10 on the holdout);
- cost/d per era and session;
- the realised FLAT payoff per era;
- the dual-touch rate;
- bootstrapped 95th-percentile drawdown. M06 DD numbers are still TBD; the reference is ≤ 50% of the user's max-DD.

**Certification:** certified by agent under owner delegation, 2026-11-28. **Rationale:** in the rationale column, and in the G2 block. No gate was lowered:
- G2 keeps its 0.02 materiality, and is restated so that a perfectly calibrated model can pass at the actual n_eff;
- G4 and G5 are restated on daily P&L;
- G1's fold count becomes a rule.

## 6. Pre-run measurements, barrier width and the viability rule

**B0's horizon is pinned at H1.** The barrier width k is chosen in this pre-run step from a pre-declared grid, where d = k·ATR_M15 and k = `TARGET_ATR`. **Choosing k is not a counted config.**

**Where it runs:**
- Viability is judged on the **most recent development era**: the part of the era 2022–end (§7) that lies before the unread slice, that is **2022-01 → 2023-11-28** (§3). It **never** runs on the holdout or on the unread slice.
- The same statistics are reported for every era.
- These are price and label statistics, with no model, so they may run on development data. They are logged in the #132 run log.
- The 2008 debug window stays for code debugging only.

**What is measured for each k in the grid {1.0, 1.25, 1.5, 2.0} at H1.** It is measured overall, per era and per session, on tradable rows only (rollover excluded):
- the FLAT share under tb.v2;
- the dual-touch rate;
- stressed cost/d (`STRESSED_COST`, §5), as a distribution: median, p75 and p90.

**Viability rule and pick rule** (pre-declared):
- **V1:** the FLAT share is ≤ **60%**.
- **V2:** the median stressed cost/d is ≤ **0.10**.
  - cost/d is computed in consistent units. On the ratio-adjusted series it is either $1.07·f(t) against the adjusted ATR, or $1.07 against the unadjusted ATR (adjusted ATR / f(t)).
  - Both give the same number, because f(t) cancels: 1.07·f(t) / (k·ATR_adj) = 1.07 / (k·ATR_adj / f(t)).
  - Mixing units, for example $1.07 against the adjusted ATR, would overstate cost/d in old history, and is not allowed.
- **Pick:** the **smallest k** in the grid that meets V1 and V2 on the most recent development era. The pick is deterministic: no other statistic enters it.
- **If no k qualifies,** v1 ends **"non-viable"**, with 0 configs spent. A v2 is then proposed: for example a longer horizon, through a Change Request, because M5/M15/H1 are the plan's primary timeframes.
- **The chosen k ships before the hash** in two forms:
  - a new label version, tb.v2 with k recorded in `label_version` (for example `m07.labels.tb.v2.k150`);
  - a `TARGET_ATR` change through risk-path review. M05's EV reads that constant. It is [PROPOSED] in `app/decision/fusion.py` on main, and moves to `app/core/bet.py` with #129.

**Driftless reference** (model-dev research F3, H1 = 4 M15 bars), with the ATR_M15 each k needs to meet V2 at $1.07/oz. From `calc.py`:

| k | FLAT share at H1 (c = 1.6 / 1.2) | ATR_M15 needed for cost/d ≤ 0.10 |
|---|---|---|
| 1.0 | 0.19 / 0.04 | $10.70 |
| 1.25 | 0.37 / 0.14 | $8.56 |
| 1.5 | 0.54 / 0.28 | $7.13 |
| 2.0 | 0.78 / 0.54 | $5.35 |

V1 and V2 pull in opposite directions: a wider barrier lowers cost/d and raises the FLAT share. Under the driftless model with c = 1.6, k = 2.0 already fails V1. So "non-viable" is a live outcome.

**Deriving the 0.10 bound** (kept as derived; not loosened):
- The targets research puts the credible traded-subset non-FLAT accuracy at 55–60% (§1.1).
- At cost/d = 0.10, reaching the PF 1.15 reference (a net per-trade edge of about 0.07; targets research §1.3) needs a gross edge p_side − p_opp ≥ 0.17.
- That means a non-FLAT accuracy ≥ 58.5% with no FLAT among the trades, and ≥ 60.6% at a 20% FLAT share. Both are at or beyond the top of the credible range.
- The bound is in units of d, so it applies at every k. Whether accuracy changes at a wider k is not modelled in v1 [L].

**Why the lever is barrier width, not horizon:**
- Inside M15–H1, moving the horizon cannot cure either trigger:
  - at a fixed k, the FLAT share can only rise as the horizon shortens (0.78 at M15, 0.49 at 30 min and 0.19 at H1, for k = 1);
  - d does not depend on the horizon.
- Master plan OD-07 leaves forecast horizons and labels to model certification, which is delegated (ADR 0002). So k can be set here.
- A horizon beyond H1 is not within that delegation, because M5/M15/H1 are the plan's primary timeframes. It needs a Change Request.

**Cost model: fixed dollars.** `STRESSED_COST` stays in fixed dollars ($1.07/oz). It is **not** scaled by price or by ATR:
- Price scaling would make the old eras cheaper, with no evidence for it. Model-dev research F3 expects cost/ATR to have been *higher* in 2016–19, not lower.
- ATR scaling would make cost/d constant, which would make V2 vacuous.
- Targets research §0.3 asserts that "Dollar ATR(M15) and dollar spread both scale with price", but cites no data. It also says: "The per-year ATR(M15) values are to be verified from data; I have not measured them." That is not evidence for era scaling.
- The consequence: in low-price eras the EV gate takes few or no trades. **An era where it takes fewer than 300 trades** (the §7 trade-tier floor) is reported as **"not covered"**, never as a pass or a fail.

**Trade rate and reachability:**
- All trade rates use **stressed cost**.
- A B0 smoke run on the 2008 debug window checks the pipeline end to end. It is code debugging, not evidence, and its trade rate is not used: 2008 says nothing about a recent holdout.
- The holdout length uses the planning rate instead (§3).
- Reachability of G3 and G4 is judged from B0's walk-forward trade rate once config #1 has run, and is recorded with the early-stop check (§8.3). If they are unreachable, record that, since it predicts "did not meet targets".

**Certification:** certified by agent under owner delegation, 2026-11-28. Coordinator decisions after an advisor review are applied here. **Rationale:**
- The FLAT share, the dual-touch rate and cost/d decide whether any config can pass. Measuring them before any budget is spent reads only price and label statistics, never model results.
- Using the most recent development era judges viability where the holdout will be, not in 2008.
- The k grid spans the widths that keep H1 within the driftless FLAT bound: k = 2.0 sits at its edge.
- The smallest-qualifying-k rule is deterministic, so no choice is made after seeing results.
- The 60% FLAT bound is about three times the driftless H1 value at k = 1.
- The 0.10 cost bound is derived above.

**v1 pre-run viability: non-viable** (provisional until it is rerun from `main` after code review).
- **Run:** 2026-11-28, on `hd-xau-v2` (HistData XAUUSD M1, DST-corrected), with the era 2022-01-01 → 2023-11-28 at H1.
- **V1** passes at k = 1.0, 1.25 and 1.5. **V2 fails at every k:** the median stressed cost/d is 0.52, 0.42, 0.35 and 0.26 against the 0.10 bound. At k = 2.0, V1 also fails (FLAT 74%).
- V2 would need k·ATR_M15 ≥ $10.70 against a median ATR_M15 of about $2.0, so k ≈ 5. At H1 that is essentially all FLAT.
- **No v1 bound was changed.** v1 spends 0 configs.
- **Provisional because:**
  - the code (749d9a7) is unreviewed;
  - the import reports `fits=False`;
  - 2023 has an unexplained 46.6% no-label rate.
- Full record: `2026-11-28-m04-v1-viability-result.md`.

## 7. Regime rule (fixed now; buckets need ≥ 300 trades and ≥ 60 trading days)

- **Volatility:** daily realised volatility as a percentile of the trailing 252 trading days. Low < 33, mid 33–66, high > 66.
- **Trend:** z-score of the 20-day return against the trailing 252-day distribution. |z| > 1 gives trend-up or trend-down; otherwise range.
- **Session (New York time):** Asia 18:00–02:00, London 02:00–08:00, New York 08:00–17:00. Rollover 17:00–18:00 is **no-trade** (the session schedule already makes it stale).
- **Era:** 2008–2015 (financial crisis, the 2011 peak and the 2013 crash; added because the GC corpus starts in 2008), 2016–2021, and 2022–end (central-bank buying above 1,000 t a year; record highs).
- **Named stress windows**, reported individually:
  - March 2020
  - 2022 rate hikes
  - April 2025 tariff shock
  - 20–21 October 2025
  - 30 January 2026

  The research verified the 2025 and 2026 dates. The others are marked unverified.
- **News windows** (tier-1 US releases ±30 min) are **not gated in v1**: no point-in-time calendar source exists yet (#41 / OD-14). This is reported as a coverage gap.
- **Adding a no-trade gate** for a weak bucket is a **config**: it spends budget and is pre-declared before it is tested.

**Certification:** certified by agent under owner delegation, 2026-11-28. **Rationale:**
- The thresholds are ex-ante observable and trailing-only. They are fixed before any result (targets research §5).
- The bucket size floors give about 80% power to detect a no-skill edge.
- **The 2008–2015 era.**
  - Its end at 2016 keeps the targets research's boundary (§5.1: 2016–2021 is range-bound).
  - 2008–2015 is a distinct regime: the financial crisis, the QE-era rally to the 2011 peak, the 2013 crash, and a downtrend into 2015. That came before the range-bound and central-bank-buying eras.
  - Calendar-year boundaries keep the rule simple and ex ante.
  - Evidence: [L]. The episode dates are well known, but they are not sourced in the research notes.

## 8. Budget, config plan, logging, terminal states

### 8.1 Counting

- **Budget:** **20 counted configs.** The counter lives in the run log.
- **Config identity.** A config is identified by a **config hash**: sha256 of the canonical JSON of its full specification:
  - label version and horizon;
  - sampling rule;
  - feature set and transform version;
  - weights (uniqueness, time decay);
  - model family and every hyperparameter;
  - seed list;
  - calibrator;
  - no-trade gates;
  - cost model.

  The code commit SHA is logged beside the hash, not inside it.
- **Before the hash.** No config counts. No model is fitted past the debug window. The only exception is the model-free pre-run statistics of §6, which read development data and never the holdout.
- **What counts.** Every run with a **new config hash** counts, whether it completes, fails or aborts. Any change to a hyperparameter, a feature or the label (any field above) is a new config.
- **What does not count** (it is still logged):
  - **The A/A seed-noise run.** This is B0 with a disjoint 5-seed list; only the seed list differs. It is pre-registered as **not a candidate**: it can never be selected or promoted.
  - **A bug-fix rerun of an identical config**, with the same config hash and a different commit. The log records the bug, the fix commit SHA and the run it voids. The voided run's results go to errata as withdrawn.
    - The fix must be applied to **every** already-run config the bug affects, and all of them are rerun. A fix therefore cannot be kept to the configs that lost.
- **N for DSR and PBO** is the budget counter: the counted configs run, failures included. Exempt runs add no trial. The A/A run is not a candidate, and a bug-fix rerun is the same trial as the run it voids.
- **Logging.** Every run is logged, failures included, with:
  - the counter value, the config hash and the commit;
  - the seeds;
  - the start and end time;
  - the status;
  - the metrics, or the failure reason.

### 8.2 Order, ranking and the win rule

- **Order.**
  - No fit on walk-forward folds before the hashed protocol record and the run log exist (#132 minimal) and the system tier (#131) is built. Before that, models run only on the debug window, and development data is read only by the model-free §6 statistics.
  - Then come the pre-run measurements and the viability rule (§6), then B0, then its A/A twin.
- **Ranking metric:** **system expectancy at stressed cost** on walk-forward out-of-sample data (the G3 statistic).
- **Hard gates:** Brier skill (G1) and ECE (G2). A config that fails either cannot be selected, whatever its expectancy.
- **Win rule.** An arm **wins** only if its stressed-cost system expectancy beats B0's, paired on the same folds, seeds and days, by **more than the A/A noise floor**. The noise floor is |B0 − A/A twin| on the same metric. A smaller difference is "unconfirmed". Winners are merged only in the explicit combination configs (#8, #12).
- **Backtest per config: decided 2026-11-28** (certified by agent under owner delegation).
  - **Ranking proxy, pre-registered:** every config is ranked by a **vectorised replay of the M05 EV rule** at stressed cost (`STRESSED_COST`), with fixed ±d exits. It gives the walk-forward daily P&L for the ranking metric and for the PBO matrix (G5).
  - **The full system backtest (#131)** runs only on the **finalist**, before the holdout opens. It supplies G3, G4 and G6 for that config. If the finalist fails there, the next-ranked config that passes G1–G2 is backtested in full, and each such run counts.
  - **Why:** measured throughput (#128, one synthetic month, extrapolated linearly over about 209 development months). The full backtest takes about 34 s a month, so about 2 hours per config (about 2.5 hours with the random baseline). Twenty configs would take about 40 hours. The proxy has no M06 path and runs in minutes.
  - **Where it runs:** the recorder backfill (about 175 s a month, so about 10 hours over the development range) and the full pipeline run on **Colab CPU high-mem** (#134), not on the development laptop. The laptop has about 8.8 GB of free disk, while the snapshot database and the JSONL export over the full range would take about 7.8 GB and 5.6 GB.
- **Selection.** The highest-ranked config that passes G1–G6 in walk-forward is the one candidate for the holdout. There is one per protocol version.

### 8.4 Holdout opening and terminal states

- **Holdout opening.** Only after a config passes G1–G6 in walk-forward, **and** the power check passes. The power check has three parts:
  - the fixed holdout is expected to reach the minimum trade count (§3) at N = counted configs run;
  - the G2 power check passes (§5);
  - the expected G3 LB is > 0 under the walk-forward effect size.
- **Terminal states:**
  - **Pass:** the holdout gates (§5) hold on the holdout. The model becomes a candidate. Before it is shadowed beside the champion and promoted to Shadow/Demo under ADR 0001, the spot-feed parity check (§1a) must pass. Without it, the claim stays about GC.
  - **Inconclusive:** either the power check fails, so the holdout is not opened; or the holdout point estimates are positive but an LB is ≤ 0. The planned next step is a Shadow extension, which is **unreachable while #17 is blocked**. Record it and stop.
  - **Fail:** a holdout point estimate is ≤ 0; or G1 fails; or the budget is exhausted in walk-forward; or the early "no edge" stop triggers; or the viability rule (§6) declares v1 non-viable. **Stop and report.** Targets are never lowered.
- **v2 rule.** A v2 after a v1 failure may only be judged on data **nobody read under v1**. Without a forward feed (#17), a v1 failure means stop and report. It does not mean retrain on the same history.

**Certification:** certified by agent under owner delegation, 2026-11-28. **Rationale:**
- The counting rules close two loopholes the research flagged: selective bug fixing, and ambiguous trial counts.
- The config plan is the research's ranking, minus the arms that need a change outside the protocol.
- The win rule and the early stop keep N, and with it the DSR penalty, as low as the evidence allows.

## 9. Promotion and kill switches (Shadow/Demo; feed OD-08 for Live)

- **Stages:**
  - Shadow: ≥ 3 months and ≥ 300 signals.
  - Demo: ≥ 3 months and ≥ 300 trades. Demo can only rule out a fake edge; proving an edge after the haircut would need about 5,000 trades.
  - Live: owner only (OD-08).
- **Kill switches** (on top of the M06 lock; never auto-resume):
  - drawdown beyond the bootstrapped 95th percentile, or underwater longer than 3× the expected time;

  - realised edge below cost/d;
  - realised costs above 1.5× the Demo-measured cost: the spread measured on the Demo feed (#17) over a window declared before promotion, plus slippage and commission. **TBD until Demo**, by this rule;
  - feature drift PSI > 0.25.

A v2 needs a stated reason, and must be judged on data no v1 run has used for claims. Viability was measured on development data (2022–2023), so those years count as read for viability purposes. Any v2 viability rule must be pre-declared before it is applied to them.
